# Decision Graph

The **Decision Graph** is QuantAI’s long-term proprietary data asset: a living map of options, signals, decisions, and outcomes.

Feeds expire. Models can be rented. The graph compounds.

---

## Why a graph

A decision is not a row in a search cache. It is a relationship among:

- A human intent (and evolving constraints)
- Candidate options (products, offers, later: other domain entities)
- Merchants / sources
- Signals at a point in time (price, trust, availability, evidence quality)
- The engine’s judgment (order, labels, confidence, gates)
- What happened next (clicked, saved, waited, bought, regretted, returned)

Those relationships are naturally a graph. Tables store pieces. The **Decision Graph** is the institutional memory that makes QuantAI smarter than a stateless wrapper.

---

## Core node types

| Node | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Intent** | Canonicalized goal for a decision episode |
| **Option** | A candidate entity (e.g. product identity) |
| **Offer** | A buyable instance (merchant, price, condition, URL) |
| **Source** | Merchant / feed / evidence origin |
| **Signal snapshot** | Measured attributes at time *t* |
| **Decision** | Engine output for an episode (order + calibration) |
| **Record** | Auditable breakdown of why |
| **Outcome** | Observed aftermath (with consent / instrumentation) |
| **Preference** | Durable user constraints and tastes |
| **Domain pack** | Laws and priors for a decision domain |

Edges capture identity (“same SKU”), competition (“alternative to”), trust (“source reliability”), causation (“signal contributed to rank”), and consequence (“decision led to outcome”).

## Network effects inside the graph

True network effects appear when more usage improves the product for everyone (with privacy boundaries):

1. **Outcome density** — more resolved decisions → better global calibration  
2. **Trust density** — more source interactions → sharper merchant/source priors  
3. **Identity density** — more observations per SKU → cleaner option nodes  
4. **Domain density** — more episodes per category → stronger packs  
5. **Platform density** (later) — B2B/API clients contribute volume under contract, improving consumer quality and vice versa  

Early on, effects are weak. The strategy is to **instrument for them from day one** so years of usage become an unassailable corpus — not to claim a flywheel that does not yet exist.

---

## Privacy and agency law for the graph

The graph is powerful; it must stay legitimate:

- Prefer aggregated and consented learning over intimate surveillance
- Separate personal preference nodes from global priors
- Never sell decision vulnerability as an ad targeting product
- Allow users to export or erase personal graph slices where law and ethics require
- Treat outcome collection as a privilege, not an entitlement

A Decision Graph that users distrust is worthless — no matter how predictive.

## Graph maturity stages

| Stage | State |
|-------|-------|
| **0 — Seeds** | Observations, RDR, saves, trust priors (today) |
| **1 — Unified model** | One conceptual schema linking episode → decision → outcome |
| **2 — Learning loops** | Gated offline improvement of priors and calibration |
| **3 — Multi-domain** | Shared graph laws; domain-specific option types |
| **4 — Platform graph** | Partner-contributed episodes under clear contracts |

Do not skip stages. A pretty ontology without outcome loops is documentation, not a moat.

---

## What the Decision Graph is not

- Not a proprietary product catalog competing with merchants
- Not a social graph for engagement
- Not a surveillance dossier
- Not a replacement for upstream discovery feeds

It is the **memory of judgment**.

---

## Strategic consequence

If QuantAI ever has a crown jewel beyond the Decision Engine’s code, it will be this:

**the Decision Graph of how the world chose — and what happened next.**

Defend it. Grow it carefully. Never mortgage it for short-term distribution.
